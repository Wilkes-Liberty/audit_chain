---
section: Security
---
The RFC 3161 witness now checks that an OCSP answer is actually about the TSA signer and comes from a responder allowed to speak for it. The response must carry a CertID matching the signer's serial, issuer Name hash, and issuer key hash; it must be signed by the issuing CA or by a delegated responder that the same CA issued with `id-kp-OCSPSigning`; and it must be current (`thisUpdate`/`producedAt` not in the future and `nextUpdate` not passed, with five minutes of clock skew, or no older than an hour when there is no `nextUpdate`). Previously any certificate chaining to a configured trust anchor could sign a "good" answer, and the first SingleResponse was used whatever certificate it named. The stored answer is judged against the token genTime; anything else, including an unknown status, fails closed. The new `witness_rfc3161.ocsp_nonce` setting (off by default, added by update 10009) sends an RFC 8954 nonce and rejects answers that do not echo it. The issuer is no longer guessed when no trust anchor signed the TSA certificate.
